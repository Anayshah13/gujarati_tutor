=== Culture and Idioms in Gujarat ===

Common Idioms / Kahevat (કહેવતો)
1. પારકી આશ સદા નિરાશ (Parki aash sada nirash)
Meaning: Relying on others always leads to disappointment. Do your job yourself.

2. જાજા હાથ રળિયામણા (Jaja hath raliyamana)
Meaning: Many hands make the work beautiful. It is similar to "Many hands make light work" in English. Collaboration brings success.

3. બોલે તેના બોર વેચાય (Bole tena bor vechay)
Meaning: Only those who communicate their skills or products can sell them. Speaking up works.

Cultural Etiquette Expressions
- In Gujarati culture, greeting elders starts with "જય શ્રી કૃષ્ણ" (Jai Shri Krishna) which has evolved beyond religion into a polite cultural greeting.
- Paternal uncle is "કાકા" (Kaka), paternal aunt "ફોઈ" (Foi), maternal uncle "મામા" (Mama) and maternal aunt "માસી" (Masi).
- A respectful way to address older people who are not related to you is by adding "ભાઈ" (Bhai - brother) or "બેન" (Ben - sister) to their names, e.g. Ramesh-bhai, Geeta-ben.
- Hospitality (અતિથિ દેવો ભવ - Atithi Devo Bhava) is extreme. Declining an offer of tea or snacks when visiting a Gujarati house multiple times is customary and offering it repeatedly is a host's duty. The word "આગ્રહ" (Aagrah - polite insistence) describes this.

Understanding Affection Words
- દીકરો (Dikro) means son, and દીકરી (Dikri) means daughter.
- Parents will often address both sons and daughters warmly as "બેટા" (Beta) or "દીકરા" (Dikra). It invokes endearment.
- "વાહલા" (Vahla) means dear beloved.
